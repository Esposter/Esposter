# Scenes

Read when a 3D screen is derived from the game's own assets, arranged, posed or judged against its references. Which tool answers which of a scene's unknowns is `references/toolbox.md`; the protocol for an unknown no tool answers yet is the `recreation-tooling` skill.

- **A scene is judged by its shape and its tone.** A 3D screen rebuilt from shapes never matches the game pixel for pixel, so `compare`'s `shape` (an F-score of the edges both share, 1 identical) and `tone` (blurred colour, 0 identical) are its numbers; the mean is read for a flat screen. A reference sets the props its state needs (`props`, a time of day), so one screen serves several references.
- **A scene is derived by the scene-derivation loop, never tuned on its frame.** Inventory everything the game draws it with before building, calibrate the tone mapping, exposure, grade and sky before any material, price each stand-in of ours by the loss its swap costs before closing one, and close the largest first with the least representation that closes it (the exact value, a fitted generator, a richer kit). A kit's few parameters are a ceiling, not a target, and a light searched until the frame's mean drops is a missing texture hidden (`apps/web/content/docs/proposals/genshin/scene-derivation.md`).
- **Read the scene's tree before its arrangement.** A root at the origin, an empty node with no children and no renderer, or a father or child no dump holds is the arrangement's first question, and `genshin:assets tree` flags each before any render. An empty anchor under a script's object (the login's `SceneBeginNode` under `SceneObj`) is where that script spawns a prefab at run time, so the arrangement is the prefab at the anchor, never a guess among the roots the blocks lay out.
- **Check the arrangement before the camera, by ratios no camera changes.** Two widths measured along the one line where their parts meet (the door over the walkway at its foot) mark four ends on that line, and a projection keeps a line's cross-ratio, so the four ends' cross-ratio holds from any pose. Their plain ratio does not: a receding line puts its ends at unequal depths, so that ratio survives only a square-on view of the line. Meeting alone is not enough either: a width measured away from the contact, like any pair of parts apart, shares no line with the other, so it is no check before a pose is solved. Measure the cross-ratio on the reference and hold it in a unit test beside the fitted data before searching for a pose; a camera search absorbs a wrong scale into a wrong pose and scores well. A root dumped at the origin (its parent in a block not read) lost its parent's scale as well as its place: find the parent in the screen's own root (`LoginScene`'s nodes), and set it as `rootParents`, never a bare offset.
- **The world's shapes come from the game's own assets, measured and never shipped.** `pnpm -C scripts genshin:assets map`, then `extract <component>`, then `fit <component>`, with the game closed: the steps, what an export holds and each fit are `apps/web/content/docs/genshin/derived-assets.md`. Nothing extracted enters the repository or a build, and no vertex or pixel of it is kept; `fit` writes only our representation's parameters, as `packages/genshin-world/src/data/<component>/*.json`, which a scene reads and never hand-edits. Adding a component takes a name pattern in `DerivedAssetComponentMap` and a fit in `DerivedAssetFitMap`. The interface is the exception, rebuilt from screenshots and recordings, which its overlay compares hold exactly.
- **A component is its arrangement, not its meshes' names.** One set of meshes is laid out several times across a scene's blocks, so a fit keeps only the roots the component is (`readComponentPlacements`), and a scene's sky, effects and prefab parts are reached from its roots by `extract`, every pointer held as its file and path ID (the page's "Following a scene's pointers"). A layout that places a part nowhere the captures show it is the wrong arrangement or the wrong heading, before it is a missing asset.
- **Read a texture's channels before fitting it.** They are rarely colours: a gradient can be falloff curves and an atlas's red the painted light. A diffuse texture is albedo, so light is measured over it, never baked into it.
- **What the exports cannot hold is measured from the captures, over what they can.** A MonoBehaviour's JSON export and a particle system carry no fields, so a camera path, an emitter's spread or a light's strength is read first from the script's raw serialized bytes where a pointer or a value's shape identifies it (`genshin:assets behaviours`), and otherwise off the references, expressed over a fitted value (the camera's height over the walkway's width, a light's strength over the stone's albedo), never as a bare figure. A scalar is measured over the region it governs (a light's strength over a lit patch of the stone, both sides sampled with `measure`), never by a search of the whole frame's mean: that mean rewards a washed-out frame over the hazy references, and a search of it lifted every light several times over while the towers lost their shading.
- **`fit` never reads the game.** `extract` is the one step that runs AnimeStudio, and it refuses while the game runs; a change of fit, selection or tolerance reruns `fit` alone.
- **A scene's camera is matched on the witness, never tuned on the frame.** `genshin:assets witness <component>` lays the exports out, `compare <reference> --witness <component>` draws them through the scene, and a pose is read off parts of known size by perspective first: their pixel widths give the focal length and distance, their rows below the horizon the eye's height, and a horizon below the frame's middle means the camera looks up. `solve-camera --iterations 0` renders that one pose to check; its grid and simplex are for refining, since the line distance barely separates heights. Read the solve's `<reference>.camera.png` before trusting its number, and treat two poses that render the same as a broken view, never a result.
- **A long solve holds the page open**, so nothing edits the world package or rebases the tree under it (`ai:queue:push` rebases in place when the tree is clean) until it ends. Stopping its background task leaves the `tsx` child running beside the next solve, both writing one log; stop a solve by its process tree (every `node` whose command line holds its `solve-camera` arguments).
