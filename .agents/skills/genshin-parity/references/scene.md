# Scenes

Read when a 3D screen is derived from the game's own assets, arranged, posed or judged against its references. A scene is rebuilt in the `recreation-tooling` skill's passes (`references/passes.md` there), each held to its own measure and then frozen, and never tuned on its frame; the passes' gates and the measures still missing are `apps/web/content/docs/proposals/genshin/recreation-passes.md`. Which tool answers which unknown is `references/toolbox.md`.

## Every pass

- **A scene is derived in passes, never tuned on its frame.** Inventory everything the game draws it with before building; settle where it stands, the camera, its shapes, its motion and its surfaces against the game's own exports; then the display transform, the light and the air off the recordings; and close each pass's largest loss first with the least representation that closes it (the exact value, a fitted generator, a richer kit), as `apps/web/content/docs/genshin/scene-derivation.md` chooses one. A kit's few parameters are a ceiling, not a target, and a light searched until the frame's mean drops is a missing texture hidden.
- **The world's shapes come from the game's own assets, measured and never shipped.** `pnpm -C scripts genshin:assets map`, then `extract <component>`, then `fit <component>`, with the game closed: the steps, what an export holds and each fit are `apps/web/content/docs/genshin/derived-assets.md`. Nothing extracted enters the repository or a build, and no vertex or pixel of it is kept; `fit` writes only our representation's parameters, as `packages/genshin-world/src/data/<component>/*.json`, which a scene reads and never hand-edits. Adding a component takes a name pattern in `DerivedAssetComponentMap` and a fit in `DerivedAssetFitMap`. A region's open world is never rooted at its capital's city index, which holds no game object: `extract` derives it from the capital's catalogued place (`apps/web/content/docs/genshin/scene-derivation.md`, "The other regions"), and its city blob is the one `genshin:assets city-areas` finds by place: that step runs once per game version, before the extracts, and its file is read at extract time. The interface is the exception, rebuilt from screenshots and recordings, which its overlay compares hold exactly.
- **`fit` never reads the game.** `extract` is the one step that runs AnimeStudio, and it refuses while the game runs; a change of fit, selection or tolerance reruns `fit` alone.
- **A tool reads a reference over its scored region only.** `openWitnessPage` hands every witness tool the reference's `checkIsScored`, so a recording's masked rows or a corner button are never read as sky or stone.
- **A long solve holds the page open**, so nothing edits the world package or moves the branch under it (`ai:queue:push` rebases in place when the tree is clean, and syncs the branch after every push) until it ends. Stopping its background task leaves the `tsx` child running beside the next solve, both writing one log; stop a solve by its process tree (every `node` whose command line holds its `pose` or `track` arguments).

## Inventory

- **Read the scene's tree before its arrangement.** A top whose whole subtree stands at the origin, an empty node with no children and no renderer, or a father or child no dump holds is the arrangement's first question, and `genshin:assets tree` flags each before any render. A collapsed top is an unplaced spawn: the witness drops it and `tree` counts it on its own line, so it never reads as an arrangement to solve. An empty anchor under a script's object (the login's `SceneBeginNode` under `SceneObj`) is where that script spawns a prefab at run time, so the arrangement is the prefab at the anchor, never a guess among the roots the blocks lay out.
- **A component is its arrangement, not its meshes' names.** One set of meshes is laid out several times across a scene's blocks, so a fit keeps only the roots the component is (`readComponentPlacements`), and a scene's sky, effects and prefab parts are reached from its roots by `extract`, every pointer held as its file and path ID (the page's "Following a scene's pointers"). A layout that places a part nowhere the captures show it is the wrong arrangement or the wrong heading, before it is a missing asset.
- **A material's value counts only where its shader's variant reads it.** `_Cutoff` stands at 0.5 on nearly every exported material as Unity's default while only the foliage shader clips by it, so the witness keys a clip on the shader or the stone's `_ENABLEALPHATEST_ON`, never on the property's presence.
- **A texture's statistics are carried in its own metres per texel.** A terrain base map's texel spans 2 m where a mesh's spans centimetres, so octaves read off one drawn at one constant scale land 50 times too fine: the ground's detail read a structure of 207 against a gate of 0.043 that way.
- **Read a texture's channels before fitting it.** They are rarely colours: a gradient can be falloff curves and an atlas's red the painted light. A diffuse texture is albedo, so light is measured over it, never baked into it.

## Layout

- **Check the arrangement before the camera, by ratios no camera changes.** Two widths measured along the one line where their parts meet (the door over the walkway at its foot) mark four ends on that line, and a projection keeps a line's cross-ratio, so the four ends' cross-ratio holds from any pose. Their plain ratio does not: a receding line puts its ends at unequal depths, so that ratio survives only a square-on view of the line. Meeting alone is not enough either: a width measured away from the contact, like any pair of parts apart, shares no line with the other, so it is no check before a pose is solved. Measure the four ends on the reference and declare them in `DerivedAssetArrangementMap`, whose test holds each cross-ratio in the fitted data and which the layout pass of `genshin:parity passes` reads beside each family's drift from the exports, before any pose; a camera search absorbs a wrong scale into a wrong pose and scores well. A prefab's root at the origin is placed by what spawns it: find the anchor and the prefab in the script's pointers (`behaviours`) and set them as the component's `spawns`, never a bare offset, so it composes through the anchor's scale as well as its place.
- **A landmark stands at its export root's height, and a shape miss is split before it is read as shape.** A landmark's `heightOffset` is fitted from its export's root over the ground (`fitRegionLandmarks`), never set by hand, and the layout pass holds each landmark's root where the scene stands it (`getWorldHeight` plus its `heightOffset`) to 5 cm of its export's, so a root stood under its export fails the pass. Windrise's statue stood 0.97 m under its export on a hand offset and read as 8 of its 10.8 px outline before that gate; dump the shape targets, split each family's outline by what the other side draws, and shift-fit its mask first.
- **An offset the exact data cannot explain holds the layout red.** A row or a part moved off where the blocks lay it to land on a frame (the login's towers' row) is a camera, an arrangement or a spawn not yet read, and the pass is not done until that is found; the offset is the question, never the answer.
- **A frame of a world scrolling past a still camera is held at its own moment.** The login's title is one camera with its rows gliding by, so each title frame is scored with the screen held where its rows stand (`heldScrolled`): `view` ours beside the exports, held, every few metres of the loop; `parts` to name the near parts on the frame; then `compare` at every metre round the match, its peak checked by eye. Edge distance alone does not find it: it rewards drawing nothing near, and a refinement on it runs a row's height away by metres. A frame with no near part outscores a near part a few metres off, so the score's peak is confirmed where the frame's near parts stand.

## Camera

- **A scene's camera is solved on the witness, never tuned on the frame.** `genshin:assets witness <component>` lays the exports out; name each landmark once in `DerivedAssetLandmarkMap` and the pixels each reference sees it at in its `landmarks`, then `genshin:parity pose <reference> --witness <component>` solves the pose and prints each landmark's reprojection error, and `--write` sets it as the reference's held camera where its screen takes one (the world's `cameraPose`), only unrefined and within the reference's pose bar, which `apps/web/content/docs/genshin/parity.md` gives as the camera pass's gate; the login's is its scene's own constants. A reference gets its own `poseBar` only once its rigid landmarks are exhausted (every rigid part the tile data can name is already one of them, and the rest still miss the gate), and always with its reason written beside the entry; the camera pass keeps the gate, so any misfit the camera carries shows there. Under six landmarks give a start and `--hold fov` at a field of view read off two widths (it does not depend on scale), then `--refine` on the families' silhouettes (held axes stay held). Parts seen in one frame share its camera: hold an axis one part's landmarks fix poorly at the value another's fix well (the door's pitch from the walkway's wings), and a run-time placement measured that way goes in the spawn's `position`; read `pose/<reference>.png` (given blue, snapped green, projected red) and `overlay --pose` before trusting the number. A pose that lands one family and leaves another with no boundary in view is two arrangements, not one pose. `track` carries a solved pose across a recording.
- A landmark is rigid architecture that no build moves, never decoration: Windrise's paving slabs (`Area_Common_Build_Ruin_H_06/07`) were loose stones whose export pair ran 33% wider than the reference shows and 8° off it, and with them in the statue's landmarks missed by 8–11 px.
- **A value read off one recording is trusted once another agrees.** A field of view, an hour or a light solved on one recording is cross-checked by solving it again on another of the same place, an older build's included wherever the place's data is unchanged. A refinement on a family's edges is kept only while the landmarks still hold: an edge refinement can slide onto a neighbour's edges (Windrise's paving onto other stones) and score better while its landmarks drift by a hundred pixels.

## Shape and surface

- **Ours reaches the shape pass only through the scene's family marks.** The shape pass draws ours of a family from the objects the scene marks with `SCENE_FAMILY_KEY` (the login's walkway, door, towers and bridges; Windrise's ground, oak and statue), so a family its scene never marks has no pixels of ours and reads no overlap, which halts the pass. A family its scene draws but never marks reads so too. Mark a family when its scene draws it, never when the pass needs a number.
- **A stand-in is judged against its own exports, at the references' cameras.** Its shape by its outline, depth and normal and its surface by its unlit colour and its glow, drawn beside the exports at the reference's camera and moment, where every pixel lines up and only the stand-in differs, and gated at what the reference can show. `rank`'s second table is that measure today, FLIP and structural similarity per family, and its first table orders the stand-ins inside the pass by what each could recover of the frame. The exports ride the scene's own motion as its parts do (the login's row, its walkway's copies, its door only where ours stands), so a frame held mid-loop compares like with like. Its eye check is a crop of the frame, ours and the exports side by side. The exports read a material as the witness reads it, so where both share a misreading they agree while the frame is wrong: every change to how a stand-in looks is read against the reference too, on its family's row of `compare`'s layers table, and looked at, before it ships.

## Light and air

- **What the exports cannot hold is measured from the captures, over what they can.** A MonoBehaviour's JSON export and a particle system carry no fields, so a camera path, an emitter's spread or a light's strength is read first from the script's raw serialized bytes where a pointer or a value's shape identifies it (`genshin:assets behaviours`), and otherwise off the references, expressed over a fitted value (the camera's height over the walkway's width, a light's strength over the stone's albedo), never as a bare figure. A scalar is measured over the region it governs (a light's strength over a lit patch of the stone, both sides sampled with `measure`), never by a search of the whole frame's mean: that mean rewards a washed-out frame over the hazy references, and a search of it lifted every light several times over while the towers lost their shading.
- **A light or a haze that has to go wrong to match reopens an earlier pass** (the `recreation-tooling` skill's `references/passes.md`): the login's day "far stone too bright" was two towers standing in the recording's sky, and `rank`'s light map is where such a cell is found on the frame.
- **A glow's scale is fitted where its colour moves, and a flat colour is a floor.** A glow whose peak stays under its material's draw range (`glowRange`) draws no pixel, so its colour reads flat from none up to that point: a scale fitted across that stretch reads the surface under the glow, not the glow. When the colour stays flat, the glow's share is the frame's own ratio, and what remains past it belongs to the surface pass.
- **A solved setting ships when no gate it holds fails and every reading past its gate moves nearer**, read from the scene's own state after it is written (a colour rounds to a byte), and never on a setting outside the range the game's own takes: a solver runs a free setting past it (a cloud coverage below none darkened the dusk's clouds rather than drawing a layer) and that win is no measurement. What a pass reads must see what a setting moves: the cloud statistics read brightness alone until the clouds' colour was added, and a solve freely turned a shade magenta.

## Acceptance

- **A scene is accepted by its shape and its tone.** A 3D screen rebuilt from shapes never matches the game pixel for pixel, so `compare`'s `shape` (an F-score of the edges both share, 1 identical), `tone` (blurred colour, 0 identical) and FLIP are its numbers once every pass holds; the mean is read for a flat screen. A reference sets the props its state needs (`props`, a time of day), so one screen serves several references.
