# Genius Invokation TCG

The card game's rules engine is built, so [Genius Invokation TCG](../../specs/genshin/genius-invokation/README.md) is what stands. This proposal is what is left of it: each card's module, the duel screen opened from a resident's talk, and the duels, decks and rewards the game gives beyond the tutorial. The talk it is opened from is the [dialogue](../../specs/genshin/dialogue/design.md)'s, which is built.

## Decisions

- **Each card's effect is a module.** The game keeps its cards' behaviour in configs under `BinOutput/GCG`, which are written as each card's module over the engine's shared effects, from the card's own description where the configs are not read, as a character's kit is. A skill that names a shared effect, such as `Effect_Damage_<element>_<n>`, needs no module of its own; a character's own `Char_Skill_<id>` script is one.
- **Every card is the game's own row.** Characters, skills, costs and cards come from the `GCGCharExcelConfigData`, `GCGSkillExcelConfigData`, `GCGCostExcelConfigData` and `GCGCardExcelConfigData` tables, and names and descriptions are the game's by text id.
- **Duels are against the game's opponents.** The residents the game seats for an Invitational and the tavern's challengers play the decks `GCGDeckExcelConfigData` gives them. Winning earns their cards and Lucky Coins, the Card Shop sells cards for Lucky Coins, and the player's Player Level grows by duels, as the game earns each.
- **Opened at Adventure Rank 32 with its tutorial quest**, as the wiki gives it.
- **The Card Shop sells in Lucky Coins.** Shop 217's goods are filed under shop type 2000 and numbered from 217000, and every one is priced in Lucky Coins (107021). The bag holds Lucky Coins as a material, so `buyShopGood` pays them from the bag with no change to the purchase rule. Its 226 goods that sell no card are published with the cards, since the shop sells them in the same coin.
- **The Player Level is the game's level table.** `GCGLevelExcelConfigData` holds ten levels, each with the EXP that passes it (the tenth passes none) and its reward id. A player who has finished the tutorial quest holds level one at no EXP. A challenger's games open by the level its week row names: Marjorie's `levelCondList` opens game 1021 at level 1 and the next four at levels 3, 5, 7 and 10, so a challenger offers each game once the Player Level reaches its level. The ladder is read as the Friendship Levels' is, by one shared rule, never a twin of it.
- **The rewards are the dump's, checked against the wiki.** The EXP each Player Level passes is `GCGLevelExcelConfigData`'s `exp`, 300, 750, 950, 1300, 1450, 1600, 1750, 1900 and 2450, and `toGcgLevels` sums them to the totals the wiki's Level Requirements table gives: 300, 1050, 2000, 3300, 4750, 6350, 8100, 10000 and 12450. The wiki's section is on the Genius Invokation TCG page, since no Player Level page exists. Level one and two's rewards are reward rows 4201001 and 4201002 (Primogem 60, Match Invitation Letter 1, Lucky Coin 500, then a card back), matching the wiki's Level Rewards table. A weekly guest's Lucky Coins are the field `INNJCJFOJCF` of `GCGWeekReward` at challenger levels 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 (300, 600, 900, 1200 and 1500), with Player EXP 200 at 1, 3, 5 and 7, matching the wiki's weekly rewards template row by row. The template labels its first row Player Level 2 where the dump keys it at challenger level 1. Three other count fields in the same rows match no wiki table and are not used. Game 13105, which no week row names, pays its victory through `GCGGameReward`: reward 4212028, Player EXP 240 and Lucky Coin 250. None of the 535 games the week rows name carries a victory reward id, so their victories are read from `GCGWeekReward` instead.
- **The card game's EXP is a save slice, and its backfill is its shape.** `cardGameExp` is a required non-negative integer on the save, the EXP the Player Level is read from, merged by the larger copy as `adventureExp` is. Its backfill, stated and not run: every stored save is written with `cardGameExp: 0`, dev first and then prod straight after, under the `backfills` skill. No stored save holds more, since no duel pays EXP until the reward path of task 4.1 lands, and a save whose tutorial quest is finished reads Player Level one at no EXP.
- **Seating is the scene's own point, as a shopkeeper's is.** The challengers are absent from the dump's NPC birth records: none of NPC 9701, 9704, 9712 or 9715 is among the NPCs the open world places, so the [scene group record](../../specs/genshin/scene-groups/design.md) holds no member for any of them. Each seat takes a point the scene does name, as a vendor's building point does, and is provisional where the wiki's location is not the point itself:
  - **Marjorie** keeps the Souvenir shop, whose Adventure Challenge the wiki gives in her shop's dialogue, so her duel (game 1021) sits on her shop row, NPC 1435, which the builder already places at that point. NPC 9701 is not seated beside it, since two residents would stand at one point.
  - **Ellin** (NPC 9704, game 1031) is placed at the Knights of Favonius headquarters' point, building 54. The wiki places her behind it, an offset no point gives, so the point is provisional.
  - **Kimiya** (NPC 9715, game 1104, the game his week row opens first among the two built) is placed beside Sumeru's crafting bench, building 526, moved as the Mondstadt keeper is. The wiki places him next to the bench on Treasures Street; building 526 is the only bench the scene holds in Sumeru. Game 1105 is not offered, since a resident holds one duel game.
  - **Furuya Noboru** (NPC 9712, game 13105), **Patton** (NPC 9700, games 1011 and 1012) and **Kurisu** (NPC 9710, game 1083) are not seated. Their wiki locations name a city (Inazuma City, Ritou) or the outside of a tavern whose point is its bartender's, so no point the scene holds is theirs.
- **A further deck is a record of the hosted game data.** Each is a `gcg/deck<id>` record and more card text in every language, published by `genshin:assets gcg` and `genshin:text gcg`, with an entry of `GcgDeckLoaderMap` reading it by key. Its card modules are code and are written with the deck they serve.
- **A deck's created cards are the ones its skills name.** The dump has no field for them, so `GcgDeckIdCreatedCardIdsMap` takes them from the `$[C<id>]` tokens in the skill text of the deck's characters and its cards. Decks 11105 and 12005 take that set. The nine decks built earlier keep their hand-picked sets, which agree with the derived set for 1, 3, 7, 11002, 30111 and 30112. For 2, 4 and 11005 the set is the derived one plus the Oceanic Mimics `122011` to `122013`: the Oceanid Mimic `122010` that the skills name is summoned as one of those three kinds (its text lists them; Rhodeia's summons `Char_Skill_22012` and `Char_Skill_22013` make them through `summonGcgOceanicMimics`). Deck 4 also takes Niwabi Enshou and Aurous Blaze, which Yoimiya's skills name, and deck 11005 takes Cat-Claw Shield `111021`, which Diona's Icy Paws names.
- **A summon's end-phase piercing is a card field.** `endPhaseStandbyPiercing` on a card module is dealt by the end-phase runner before the card's own hook damage, so a card module never imports the damage path, which imports the card map.
- **A card that switches a character in takes the switch path.** `switchOnPlay` on a card module is resolved by the duel after the card's `play` hook and before its skill, through `switchGcgActiveCharacter`: the field's switch hooks fire, but no die is paid and no turn passes, because the card's own cost is what it pays.
- **A talent card runs its own skill script.** Lightning Stiletto runs `Char_Skill_14032_Ex_1`, the card's own script, which grants Keqing Electro Infusion and creates no further Lightning Stiletto, while Keqing's Stellar Restoration (`Char_Skill_14032`) creates one in hand, or, with a Lightning Stiletto already in hand, discards every copy there and grants Electro Infusion. The Electro Infusion is the status card 114032 (provisional: the card text's `$[K1002]` token is not resolved in the dump).
- **No duel against another player.** Duels with friends need other players, which [co-op](../../deferred/genshin/co-op/design.md) defers; the game gives them no reward either.
- **The Player Level and the Friendship Level read one rule.** `computeLevelReached` counts the levels an EXP total has reached over any table of `{ exp }` rows, so the card game's level and the companionship level are the same function.
- **The duel holds its random source.** `GcgDuel.random` is the seeded source its dice are rolled from and its decks shuffled with. The functions that roll or shuffle read it from the duel, so a caller passes none, and a card's draw or roll reads the duel's the same way. A choice a player makes when a card is played, such as the dice Toss-Up rerolls, is a typed argument on the play, which the duel screen supplies.
- **A hit reports what it dealt.** `applyGcgDamage` and `applyGcgStandbyPiercing` return a `GcgDealtDamage` for each hit they land on a standing character, with the element or kind it hit with and the reaction it triggered, and a Superconduct or Electro-Charged reaction's piercing on the other standing characters is among them. `dealGcgSkillDamage` returns its skill's hits, and `onSkillUsed` receives every hit a skill landed, its own damage and its standby piercing alike, so a card can read what the skill did.
- **A card's hook hears the character it is attached to.** `GcgEffectContext.characterIndex` names the character a card is attached to, as an equipment or a status. The end-phase runner and the skill-use runner set it, so a hook can act on its own character: an equipment's heal on its wearer, a status's heal on the character it was given to. A card on the field that no character holds carries none.
- **The side holds its deck's card ids.** `GcgSideState.deckCardIds` keeps the ids the deck was built with, copies included, so a rule that counts the deck (Timaeus's six Artifact Cards) reads the deck itself, not the distinct definitions in `cards`. Its builder takes them from the deck.
- **A card enters with the counter its module gives.** `initialCounter` is read by `createGcgZoneCard` the way `initialUsages` and `initialRounds` are. Timaeus starts with two Transmutation Materials.
- **Timaeus spends the Artifact's total cost.** Once per Round its free equip reduces the Artifact's remaining cost to none, and spends materials equal to the Artifact's total cost, read from the card. This is a provisional reading of "spend Transmutation Materials equal to the total cost".
- **A play takes the choice the duel screen gives it.** `playGcgCard` takes a `GcgPlayChoice` after the paid dice and passes it to the card's `play` as its third argument. Toss-Up's selections are the first such choice: the duel screen takes the payment, then up to two groups of dice to reroll, and each group is numbered among the dice the payment leaves (`toGcgRemainingDiceIndices`). A reroll a card makes outside the roll phase throws through `rethrowGcgDice`, the same throw as the roll phase's one reroll, so both read the duel's random source.
- **Toss-Up's rerolls count valid selections only.** A selection naming a die the side does not have is passed over and does not count toward its two rerolls.
- **A card on either side hears a skill of either side.** `onAnySkillUsed` runs for each card on each side's field, after the using side's `onSkillUsed` hooks, with the card's own side in the context, so a card that answers the opposing side's skills (Chang the Ninth) hears them. The cards that hear a skill are those on the field when it is used, as before, and both sides' zone cards are pruned after it, so a card an opposing skill spends leaves its field then.
- **Chang the Ninth counts a skill once.** Its Inspiration is one per skill that dealt Physical or Piercing DMG or triggered a reaction, however many hits it landed; at three it is discarded and its side draws two.
- **Mushroom Pizza holds two statuses.** Its event gives the target a one-round food status (333007, in the food list, so one food a Round) and the dump's Mushroom Pizza (In Effect) status (303305). The In Effect status heals its owner at each End Phase and is spent after two; it is not a food, so it does not bar food in later Rounds.
- **Lightning Stiletto is a combat action.** `GcgCardModule.isCombatAction` marks a card whose play passes the turn, as a combat skill or switch does; `playGcgCard` passes it once the card resolves. Every other card stays a fast action. The dump gives the Stiletto a Combat Action token, so it is the first card flagged.

## Scope and order

**Standing:** the [as-built page](../../specs/genshin/genius-invokation/README.md) holds the rules engine, the decks already built and the duel board, which opens from a resident's talk. Three challengers' duels are seated in the residents builder (Marjorie, Ellin and Kimiya), and their rows land with the residents run; Furuya Noboru, Patton and Kurisu have no point the scene holds. Every resident holds their standing talk, which offers a seated challenger's duel as a reply.
**This adds, in order:**

1. **The Player Level and the games it opens.** Built: `computeGcgPlayerLevel` (zero until the tutorial quest is finished, then the level its EXP reaches), `services/gcg/readGcgLevels.ts` reading `gcg/levels` with `gcgLevelsSchema`, and `buildGcgLevels`, which `genshin:assets gcg` writes from `toGcgLevels.ts`. Left: the real publish of `gcg/levels`, which the main session makes so the reader typechecks against the lock, and the tutorial's start, which waits on quest 70665 ("Come Try Genius Invokation TCG!") being carried, since the world has no finish event for it. **Publish, for the Mac:** the `gcg` command's dry run adds one record, `gcg/levels`, and moves no other.

2. **Seating the challengers**, at the scene's points (see the decision above). Marjorie (game 1021 with deck 11005) and Ellin (NPC 9704, game 1031 with deck 11002) are seated once the region that holds their residents is written. Each seated challenger's talk is their standing talk, published with every resident's by `genshin:text standing-talks`, and its opening line offers the duel reply `191219274`, so no talk is written for a seat.
3. **The other opponent decks**, the invitationals', the tavern challengers' and the rest the duel rows name, each given the modules its cards and skills need. The decks the early duels play are built, and the as-built page records them. Each is published as its `gcg/deck<id>` record and read through `GcgDeckLoaderMap`. Left: the 7.1 dump holds a card list (`cardList`, a plain name the reader reads as it is) for 783 of its 1132 decks and none for the other 349, among them the challengers' decks 12103, 13001, 14002, 12105 and 13003. No other table or field holds deck cards, so no mapping recovers them. The wiki does not either: the stage pages of Uncle Tian, Karpillia, Nasrin, Jifang and Qiuyue name each opponent's lineup of characters and no cards, and the Deck Menu page describes the player's own decks. Of the 349, 252 are all-monster decks, the shape of Ellin's 11002, whose spec says it holds no cards; the other 91 hold player characters and no list, which the dump does not explain. A deck with no list is not published with cards until those 91 are explained.
4. **Invitationals, tavern challengers, the Card Shop, and the EXP the duels pay into the Player Level.** Built: the Card Shop's slice, `shops/gcgCardShop`, takes shop 217's 330 goods, all priced in Lucky Coins (104 sell a card, and 226 sell items the card table does not hold). A bought card goes into the bag, since no collection state exists yet. Of the normal week-level challengers' 38 decks (Marjorie and Ellin excluded), 10 hold a card list and 28 hold none. Built: 11105, with every card modelled, and 12005, with its created cards modelled too (Sacred Cryo Pearl, Ice Lotus, Jade Screen, Stellar Restoration and Lightning Stiletto). Lightning Stiletto's Electro Infusion and its discard of copies already in hand are not modelled, and the duel plays it as a fast action, not the combat action the game gives it. Left: the other eight decks with lists hold 55 unmodelled cards, and their created cards name 29 more.

## Data and measures

- **Read from the game's tables:** the `GCG*` tables and the configs under `BinOutput/GCG`. The tables are fetched into the dump beside the standard rule's, and only the slices a duel loads are written into the world.
- **Read from the wiki:** each card's effect where its configs are not read, and each duel rule's detail.
- **Measured:** the duel board's layout against a whole-frame public recording of the board, which scores its tints, words and places. Its card art, ornate frame and profile plates are the game's and are not drawn, so they stay in the score until the user accepts the layout.
