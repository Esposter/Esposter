# Recreation passes

This page is the order a Genshin screen is rebuilt in, built on the [scene derivation](../../specs/genshin/scene-derivation/design.md)'s three kinds of information and its witness render, and on the [parity](../../specs/genshin/parity/README.md) loop's commands. The login scene has been worked for a long time by one loop: change something, shoot the whole frame, score it against a recording, rank every term of its error by the most it could recover, and work the largest. Each turn of that loop moves the frame by hundredths of its perceptual score, and the loop does not converge, because every unknown is judged through every other one:

- **A placement read as a haze.** By day the stone twenty metres up and some way out read twice as bright as the recording's, which the light and the haze were solved against pass after pass. The light map of `rank` found it on two towers standing where the recording shows sky: a placement that no light or haze could ever fix, absorbed by both.
- **A light traded for a haze.** Solved free, the light went below none to cancel a haze too bright over the far stone. Two unknowns landing in one score settled at whatever hid both.
- **Random content against a pixel score.** The game's cloud layer, ported from its own program and drawn over its own textures, scored the frame worse at every setting, as the particle clouds did before it: clouds the game scatters at random can never win a score comparing pixels, whatever their amount.

The interface did not have this problem. Its pieces are placed from the game's own rect tree, moved by its own clips, and compared over the recording's own frame with everything else held, so each difference points at the one piece that caused it, and it reached a few tenths of a percent in a handful of passes. This proposal runs every part of a screen the way the interface runs.

## Decisions

- **Passes in dependency order, each answering one kind of unknown.** An unknown is answered after everything it depends on and before anything that depends on it: what exists, where it stands, the camera that sees it, its shape, its motion, its surface, the transform from scene colour to the screen, the light on it, the air around it and the sounds it makes. A later pass's solve absorbs an earlier one's error, so a pass is never begun on a red pass before it.
- **Each pass has its own measure, in screen units at the references' cameras.** A placement is measured in metres against the exports' transforms and in pixels where it lands, a shape by its depth, normal and outline against the exports' drawn at the same camera, a surface by its unlit colour against theirs, a sound by its bands' envelopes against the game's own sound. No pass is judged by the frame's score, which every other pass moves too.
- **A pass is done when its measure is within the reference's own noise, and then it is frozen.** Its gate is a threshold its measure must meet on every reference, and what it produced is held by a test (as the arrangement's cross-ratios already are), so no later pass may move it to make its own numbers better. A later pass that finds an earlier one wrong reopens that pass, never compensates for it.
- **A normal detail's frequency is the reference's own surface lobe, and its amplitude is solved at that frequency from the exports' bend.** Each detail family's wavelength is the strongest lobe of its exports' normal-angle field under a cap of an eighth of the family's pixel width, a cap set before the bridges were read and kept. `readSurfaceWavelengths` reads it at the reference camera after a 16-pixel box high-pass and converts it to metres at the median depth: the towers read 2.5648 m and the bridges 21.8913 m (under their 25.4 m cap, `SURFACE_LAG_CAP_SHARE`). The detail is a height of the engine's noise, `amplitude` metres high over a cell `wavelength` metres across (`createNormalDetailNode`), so the mean lean of its normals is the amplitude times the noise's mean tangent gradient, 0.885 in the noise's lattice units (measured by sampling `computeMxPerlinNoise` at 400000 seeded points and normals). The bend the exports give fixes the amplitude, and the bend depends on the frequency, since a surface spans few of the noise's cells: the towers' bend read 0.0146 degrees off at 10 cycles a metre, 0.0592 at 0.39 and 0.0191 at 1.3. Solved at the wavelengths above, from the origin where no detail reads no bend, the first step at 0.1591 m and 0.7604 m read the towers' bend 0.0548 degrees off against 0.0198 and the bridges' 0.1646 against 0.1320; the second, at 0.1619 m and 0.8388 m, reads 0.0005 and 0.0000, both held, and the walkway holds at 0.0006. A family whose own tilt already bends more than its exports keeps the share of that tilt that brings them level, since an added detail can only raise a mean lean: the walkway, `LOGIN_WALKWAY_RIM_TILT_SHARE`.
- **A family whose frame resolves no lobe takes its wavelength from its exported normal map.** `readSurfaceWavelengths` resolves the frame's lobe first, and the family's normal map only where it resolves none (`chooseDetailWavelength`). The map is the one the family's materials reference most (`readFamilyMapWavelength`, `readTextureWavelength`): its tilt angles are read in texel space over the texels the family's UV triangles cover, the lobe is found through each high-pass box of `TEXTURE_HIGH_PASS_RADII_TEXELS` (4 to 128 texels) by `computeSurfaceLag`, the one that holds across the most boxes is kept (`chooseHeldLobe`: a lobe that moves with its box is the box's, and a tie goes to the shorter), under a lag cap of an eighth of the family's UV span in texels, and a texel spans the family's metres per UV unit over the map's width. Those metres are `computeMetresPerUv`, the median over the family's triangles of the root of the world-area over UV-area ratio, with each placement's scale: the door's placement is 0.1, so its 2.7859 metres per UV unit. The door reads 13 texels, 0.0354 m (`LOGIN_DOOR_NORMAL_DETAIL_WAVELENGTH`): the lobe holds at boxes of 4, 8 and 32 texels, against 39 at 16, 96 at 64 and 123 at 128, so it is the one the most boxes agree on, where the 16-texel box that the frame's own lobe is read through would have taken 39. The door's detail amplitude is solved at 0.0354 m from the exports' bend of 4.7203 degrees: the first step at 0.00946 m reads 13.5377 degrees, 8.8174 off, the secant through the two readings, 0.0033 m, reads 4.8477 degrees, 0.1274 off, and the secant from there, 0.00321 m, reads 0.0039 degrees off, held against 0.0198 (`LOGIN_DOOR_NORMAL_DETAIL_AMPLITUDE`). Our frame's own lobe on the door reads 0.1395 m once the detail is wired, a diagnostic only.
- **A normal detail's world normal is taken to view space by the camera alone.** `createNormalDetailNode` passed the world-space leaned normal to `transformNormalToView`, which applies the object's model normal matrix first. An object under a rotated group (the door's `[0, π, 0]`) had its normal turned a second time, a flip of about 172 degrees at any amplitude, even zero, against its geometry normal. The leaned normal now goes to view space through `cameraViewMatrix` alone. The towers, bridges and walkway, whose transforms carry no rotation, read the same as before.

- **Our render's wavelength is a diagnostic, never a gate.** Our detail has no lobe the measure can read at the reference's 14 pixels a metre: its row autocorrelation is about 0.02 at every lag, so a gate on it could not be met honestly. The family's bend gates it instead, and our wavelength is printed beside it as a diagnostic (`ParityPassDiagnostic`, its gate and held cells '-'), which reads 8.5495 m for the towers and 23.2595 m for the bridges with the detail wired, against 2.5648 m and 21.8913 m in the exports. The diagnostic is a record of the detail's field, never a value a pass solves against.
- **The dusk's delta with the detail is the Light pass's reading, and the detail stays.** With the normal detail wired, the login's screen-dusk compare reads a mean difference of 20.55 against 19.89 without it, every Shape gate holding. The detail's bends are solved from the exports, so the frame's extra 0.66 points come from the sun's angle on those normals, which no Shape gate judges. The Light pass judges that angle on the detail (its shadows' edges and the dusk sun's direction), and the detail is reverted only if that pass cannot take the dusk back below its 19.89.
- **A surface's structure is matched by its band statistics, and a flat or stand-in albedo is solved from them.** The structure reading is the root mean square of each term's relative error: the luminance's variance and each octave band's detail energy, the four bands between blurs of 1, 2, 4 and 8 pixels (`computeStructureTerms`). A family whose albedo is one colour reads every term at its full loss, so the bridges read 1.0000, and the towers' shades and the walkway's paving hold tones but not the grain. The fix is the toolbox's own route rather than shipped texture: each family's grain is our noise, its octave amplitudes fitted to the exports' band energies (the cloud layer's spectral route, `fitSpectralNoiseChannel`), and the exports' terms are printed by the surface pass's notes before each family is worked. The bridges' grain is the engine's `createMaterialDetail`, its targets the exports' terms over their mean lightness squared, scaled by the ratio the reading gave our grain's terms (0.20 to 0.26): the first fit read 0.7865 and the calibrated one 0.0544 against 0.3425, and every term holds within 0.11. The towers' grain adds to the facade's own structure, so its targets are the exports' terms less the facade's, and four calibrations of the reading bring it to 0.0179 against 0.0216 (`LOGIN_TOWER_SURFACE_DETAIL`). The door's grain goes over its relief, its variance target calibrated on its own because the solver couples the variance to the bands: five calibrations bring the structure to 0.0191 against 0.0194 (`LOGIN_DOOR_SURFACE_DETAIL`).
- **The recording's colour enters only for what exists at run time.** Layout, shape, motion a clip holds and surface are the game's own data, so they are judged against the game's own exports through the witness render, aligned to the pixel and free of any light. A recording's camera, and any motion no clip holds (a flight a script drives, the walkway's rise), live only in that recording, and are read off it through the frozen layout alone: its landmarks located in the frame and its outlines overlaid, with no colour of the recording in the reading. The display transform, the light, the atmosphere and the sounds are set at run time, and only for them is the recording's colour consulted, each pass over a mask where only its own unknown moves the reading.
- **Random content is judged by its statistics.** Clouds, wisps and anything else the game scatters is matched by its cover by height, its size and its colour spread, never by a pixel score.
- **The frame's perceptual score is acceptance.** Once every gate holds, `compare` scores each reference. A frame that still stands off names a pass whose measure missed something, which is the measure's defect to fix.
- **A stand-off names its pass from the report the passes last recorded.** On a reference that stands off, `compare` reads its component's report (`passes/snapshots`) and names the first pass in the passes' order with a row outside its gate, which is the pass reopened. The passes are not run to name it: a run of them is what re-measures the report, and `passes` writes the run's first red pass there. A compare that ran the passes on every stand-off measured the whole run above the bar on each login compare, and was slow for it. Rejected: a `--reopen` flag that runs the passes for the name, since the report already holds the run's first red pass; and mapping the worst compare cells to the pass that owns their content kind, since that needs a table from cell to pass the repo does not hold. A report with no rows names no pass and says to run `passes`. `findMovedFrozenPass.ts` names the other case, a frozen pass that a later run moves.
- **Ceilings order the work inside a pass, never across passes.** Within one pass, the items are worked largest first by what they could recover, as `rank` prices them; a term in a later pass waits for every earlier gate however large it is.
- **The layout reads a placement in pixels by its pairs, before any render.** Each fitted part beside the export nearest it, both carried as the scene carries their family in a current build's reference's state, at every copy of a row the scene repeats it along, and projected from the scene's camera there, read only where the export lands on the frame and gated at a recording's two pixels of softness. A family with no part on a reference's frame has nothing to read there and is skipped for it, said so in the pass's notes, while a family on no reference's frame at all is a miss, since no frame has placed it. The camera is the next pass's, but a camera a little off moves both points of a pair alike, so their gap reads the placement alone. A family's outline in pixels is the shape pass's, drawn at the same camera, so the layout needs none.
- **The light reads the sun's direction on its shadows' edges over flat receivers.** The receivers are the exports' parts facing up; the reference's shadow on them is its shading, its colour over the exports' albedo, split by Otsu's threshold; ours is the exports' occluders cast by the scene's own shadow map from the direction tried. The two edges are priced both ways in the reference's pixels and gated as any place on the frame is, and the direction is solved by its heading and its height over the horizon from the scene's, solved on an older build's reference seen from its own camera, itself solved on its landmarks.
- **`MHYBloom_Z`'s bloom fields are read in the order the prefilter takes them.** The profile's parameters are override and value pairs of eight bytes in field order, and its overridden values at bytes 60, 76 and 84 leave one pair between the first two, at 68, at its default. The prefilter reads `_MHYBloomThreshold`, then `_MHYBloomThresholdCharacter` where a character's pixels stand, then `_MHYBloomScaler`, and the compose `_MHYBloomIntensity` (`Login/Scene/Display.reference.ts`). So the threshold is the 0.75 at byte 60, the character threshold the default left at 68, which the login never needs since it draws no character, the scaler the 2.5 at 76 and the intensity the 0.75 at 84. The bloom's own reading confirms it once the bloom draws, and does not gate the build. The prefilter is one TSL node, `GameBloomNode`, which the pipeline runs: the CPU copy `computeBloomPrefilter.ts` had no caller beyond its own test and repeated the node's arithmetic, so it is deleted with its test and its row in `rendering-style` (task 1.5). The node's structure is its input, `GameBloomStructure` (`GameBloomNode.ts`): the prefilter's form, the downsample's tap offset and the blur's form and pass count. Its default, `GENSHIN_BLOOM_STRUCTURE`, is the shape the engine has drawn since the bloom landed, a one-tap prefilter, taps half a texel out and the separable pair once, and the table below reads every other combination. A later edit to the prefilter lands in the node alone.
- **The bloom's extent is the game's chain, not the engine's separable pair, and the frame's size does not enter it.** The bloom shader's twelve programs are `{data-root}/genshin-parity/extracted/login/shaders/12903389/Shader#90` (3Dmigoto's disassembly with its constants annotated). Read off its vertex and pixel programs, the game's chain against the engine's `GameBloomNode`:

  | Stage                 | Game (`Shader#90`)                                                                                                                                   | Engine (`GameBloomNode.ts`)                                                           |
  | :-------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
  | Prefilter             | One tap, `max(0, colour - threshold) * scaler` (`00002`)                                                                                             | The same                                                                              |
  | Downsample, per level | Four taps at ±1 source texel, equal weights (`00011`, `00010`): under bilinear filtering a four-texel box per axis                                   | Four taps at ±0.5 source texel, which land on texel centres: a two-texel box per axis |
  | Blur, per level       | Four taps at (0.96, 0.25), (0.25, -0.96), (-0.96, -0.25) and (-0.25, 0.96) texels, one rotated pass (`00000`, `00010`)                               | Two separable passes, each four taps at ±0.25 and ±0.96 texels                        |
  | Levels and compose    | Four levels by the four weights, from an atlas (`00001` with `00003`) or four textures (`00009`)                                                     | Four levels by the same weights, four textures                                        |
  | Resolution            | Not in the dumps: buffer sizes and the atlas transforms (`_MHYBloomUVTransform1..3`, `_UVTransformSource`, `_UVTransformTarget`) are run-time values | Each level halves the drawing buffer                                                  |

  In each level's own texels, the game's downsample and one blur pass give a variance of 0.83 texel², the engine's pair 1.07, so the engine's kernel is about 14% wider in sigma. Were the game to blur twice a level, its 1.32 would be about 23% above the engine's variance (11% in sigma), so the sign hangs on a count the dumps leave open, and the blur pass count is recorded as an assumption. The frame's size does not enter the extent: every level halves its frame, so each kernel covers the same share of the frame at any size, the 640-wide display and the 1080-line reference alike. What the dumps cannot settle is the level sizes the game chose, so the engine's halving is an assumption too. The one-tap prefilter is the chain the Display reference records (`00002`); the four-tap form that takes the threshold after its average (`00004`) is read in the dump and is not the chain taken here. The step-2 port took those shapes whole: the downsample at ±1 source texel and the blur as one rotated pass, each tap's texel from the game's constants at the level's own size. On the login's Display pass the port reads 1.6097 ΔE over the reference's brightest pixels against the shipped pair's 1.4989, further from the gate of 0, while the contrast holds at 0.0610 against 0.1000. It was reverted, so the engine keeps the separable pair. The game's kernel, taken whole, moves the reading the wrong way, so the extent does not name the cause of the failing reading. Those three open questions are read by the ablation in Open below: the prefilter's four-tap form (`00004`), the blur's pass count per level, and the resampling of the reference to the display width (`readRawRgb`'s lanczos3 over the reference, against our frame rendered at 640 without supersampling).

- **The display chain, stage by stage, from the game's post programs.** The bloom programs are `{data-root}/genshin-parity/extracted/login/shaders/12903389/Shader#90`, and the uber pass that adds the bloom, balances white, tones and encodes is `Shader#88` (6337 variants, of which the login draws one). Read stage by stage beside the engine (`createPostPipeline.ts`, `toneMapGenshinNode.ts`, `GameBloomNode.ts`):
  1. **Prefilter** (`Shader#90` `00002`): `max(0, scene - 0.75) * 2.5`, one tap. Engine: the same. Matches.
  2. **Levels** (`00011`, `00000`, `00010`): a four-tap box downsample and a rotated four-tap blur. Engine: the separable pair. The blur's form is the open structure above, and no combination of it holds its gate.
  3. **Compose and intensity** (`00009`, with `_MHYBloomIntensity` at byte 84, 0.75): the four levels summed by 0.3, 0.3, 0.26 and 0.15, the intensity applied once before the add. Engine: the sum times `GENSHIN_BLOOM_INTENSITY`. Matches.
  4. **Add before white balance** (uber variant `00007`: `scene + intensity * bloom`, then the balance). Engine: `sightedNode.add(bloomNode)` before `whiteBalance.mul`. Matches the order.
  5. **Auto exposure** (uber: with `_AutoExposureScale` above 0 the sum is multiplied by `1 + AE * (exposureTex - 1)`). Engine: none. The login's scale is not settled: byte 268 holds an overridden 0 that the export does not name, so the login is taken as off and not verified. Missing.
  6. **White balance** (uber: `cb0[99..102]`, the three columns of a 3x3 matrix, applied to the RGB before the curve). Engine: `computeWhiteBalance` per hour, before `toneMapGenshinNode`. The order matches. The values are not in the dumps: the script sets them on screen only, so the per-hour fit stays the only source. Missing.
  7. **Exposure** (inside the curve, `E` in `1 - 2^(-E x)`, 1.0 at byte 252). Engine: `GENSHIN_TONE_EXPOSURE` = 1. Matches.
  8. **Tone curve** (uber variant `00007` under `_MHYBloomTonemapping`: `min(1, (1 - 2^(-E x) + 0.0001)^(C + 0.01))`, with C = 0.5 at byte 260). Engine: `toneMapGenshinNode`, the same constants. Matches. The uber also holds a rational fit, `x (1.36 x + 0.047) / (x (0.93 x + 0.56) + 0.14)` (variant `03587`), which declares `_MHYBloomContrast` but never reads it. The dumps do not name the keyword state that selects one variant, so two checks choose the exp form: the profile overrides the contrast field to 0.5 and only the exp variant consumes it; and the light-plane residual the Display pass uses, which fits nothing, reads login-door-session's light flat to 1.361e-4 under the shipped exp curve (1.283e-4 at its best contrast, 0.439) against 9.74e-4 under the rational form (a one-off what-if, not committed).
  9. **Grade table** (uber `_Lut3D`, read only on the wide-range path, where `_HDR_ON` is above a half). Engine: none on the login, which sets no grading LUT (`Login/Scene/Index.vue` passes none; only Windrise does). Matches.
  10. **Encode** (uber: sRGB OETF, then raised to `_UserInputGamma`, then a dither of 1/255). Engine: `renderOutput`, which three encodes to sRGB, with no user gamma and no dither. Matches for a gamma of 1. `_UserInputGamma` is a runtime user setting the dumps do not hold, so it is missing, and the dither is omitted.

  Verdict: each stage whose form, order or constant the dumps hold matches the engine, so nothing is ported. Nothing read here explains the washed-out frame, which the Display pass leaves where it was (the contrast excess 0.0610 held, the bloom reading 1.1762 failing). Recorded as missing, not solved: the white-balance matrix per hour, `_UserInputGamma`, the auto-exposure scale behind byte 268, and the keyword state that selects the login's uber variant. The reruns on this change agree: the band table is the one above, `genshin:parity bloom` holds no row of its 32 (the lowest 1.1642), and `passes login --pass Surface` holds every row, so Display is reached and still fails its bloom reading. Task 1.1 stays open, and task 1.4's compare runs wait on it.

- **The audio pass reads each sound's bands, never its onset.** Each effect is scored against the game's sounds it was fitted from in both channels every 5 milliseconds and gated at a second take of our own noise seeded apart, the grain only the game's samples would close. Its delay after what sets it off is read once off the recording's frames and written straight into its constant, nothing solved over it, and its own rise is already its first frames' bands.

- **The exports' camera is the reference's own, never the scene's.** `solveReferenceCamera` started its landmark solve from our scene's camera and held x, y, yaw, pitch and fov there, so a change to the login's constants (`LOGIN_CAMERA_*`) moved the exports' frame, every surface gate, and the Layout pass's pixel readings. The start is now the camera the reference records (`cameraPose` on its entry, the Windrise statue's `cameraPose` prop beside it, `getReferenceCameraPose`), and the eye is solved along the glide alone from it. The login reference holds three landmarks (its door's apex and feet), which fix three of the six axes, so a six-axis solve from its landmarks alone is not determined: x, y, yaw, pitch and fov are the recorded pose, the login's fitted solve (`Camera.reference.ts`, its door's pixel widths and rows), and the constants carry the same numbers by design. A reference that records none is solved on all six axes from six landmarks or more, and throws below that. The proof on the real render: with `LOGIN_CAMERA_FOV` at 50 in place of 45, `passes login --pass Surface` reads the exports' terms, every Surface gate and the Camera reading identically to the run at 45, and `--pass Layout` reads every row identically. Layout's own pixel rows moved once, Door 0.3361 to 0.2732 px, as they now project from the reference's pose rather than the scene's pre-solve pose; every gate still holds at its 2 px bar.

- **A grain is fitted by a damped Newton over its own structure terms on the render, never by a per-term update.** The per-term update scaled each target by its own ratio, and a target's realised share moved with the others, so the finest band rose as the variance target fell and the walkway's two hand calibrations did not converge. `solveResidualsByNewton` takes the unknowns as the detail's targets (the bands and the variance, each octave amplitude set on the live material by `setSceneSurfaceAmplitudes`) and the residuals as the family's structure terms read back from the render (`computeFamilyStructure`, the same definition the surface pass gates). Its finite differences measure the response the render holds, coupling included. On a synthetic response that couples the finest band to the variance, the per-term update leaves its worst output 0.498 off after 60 steps, and the Newton solve converges in 3. On the walkway it reached a structure root mean square of 0.0214 against the 0.0268 gate, the fourth-octave target at its floor of zero, where the paving alone overfills that band, so the grain cannot close it. The door, held at 0.0191 by the calibrations, reached 0.0098 under the same solve. Towers and bridges hold their gates and were not refitted.

The interface runs beside these as a pass of its own, placed from the rect tree, moved by its clips and compared over the recording's frame, as [parity](../../specs/genshin/parity/README.md) describes.

### Why it converges

Judged through the frame, each of the scene's unknowns is confounded by every other one, and fixing one re-prices the rest, so the work grows with the square of the unknowns and the loop can stall forever on a pair of them trading error. Judged by its own measure, each unknown is solved once against data that only it moves, most of them in closed form from exact data, and frozen: the work grows with the number of unknowns, and every pass's result stays true as later passes land. The interface is the case that already runs this way.

## The first run: the login

The login runs through every pass from its inventory as its measures land, each gate holding before the next pass begins. What each pass has in question there, a row shifted by a fitted value, a camera the old recordings may not share, a rise with no measure yet, a transform left out, suns set by hand, a cloud layer waiting on its settings and the sounds still unmatched, is that pass's item on the [roadmap](../genshin-roadmap/tasks.md), in the passes' order. Windrise starts at the references the same way once the login's gates hold.

## The login's open steps

Each step is answered by its pass, in the order above, and is done once the pass's gate holds and its result is frozen.

- **Inventory.** Name every field of the login's environment and scripts (`EnviroSky`, `LoginSceneEnviro`, `LoginSceneWeather` and the rest) by matching it against what it sets, so later passes read the game's values rather than solve them from recordings. Each effect, clip and sound the exports hold is claimed by a part of ours or named as not drawn.
- **Layout.** Place the towers that stand a dozen pixels or more off: the dawn's left tower, the dusk's middle towers, the day's two wide towers top left and the lantern tower's crown. Edges cannot place them, since the day's towers fit within 10 to 12 pixels along the whole loop, so the measure needs each tower's upper sky outline held against ours. Each hour's frame is checked again on a current build's recording, as the older references were taken on older builds.
- **References.** A frame of the title at each hour, and of the door at rest, from a current build's recording.
- **Shape.** Judge each stand-in's outline, depth and normal against the exports before its surface or light. Sunk recesses of four units or more are carved as openings; shallow ones stay painted, since boxing them scored worse. Relief that tilts the light along a recess's edge needs carved geometry of our own, the crown's colonnade first, because bump maps did not move the towers against the exports.
- **Motion.** Check the walkway's two easing curves and the distances beside its rows against the current recording, and the glide's two speeds against the script.
- **Surface.** Judge each unlit colour against the exports at the references' cameras. The towers' stone needs a procedural grain of our own, fitted to scale and contrast. Gilding needs an environment map of the login's sky before it reads as metal. The walkway's surface needs its finest scale (joints, pocket rims, lane borders), its rims at the game's one-centimetre width, and the dais wings' shadow. The door's front needs its carved relief and light, the largest term on the phone's door frame.
- **Display.** The night's red needs a white balance matrix (`_WhiteBalanceMat`) before the tone curve, set per sky state, and the curve's contrast is held by the display pass on the current recording. The game's bloom fields are read by the bloom reading in Scope. Windrise's palette is tuned again under the curve.
- **Light.** Solve the light apart from the haze, over bins the haze barely reaches, and then solve the haze's colours under it. Then the falloff with height past the haze, the reflection pass's ambient with its clamp and highlight, the dusk sun's direction from its shadows' edges (`genshin:parity shadows`), and the phone door frame's light under the PC client's day light.
- **Atmosphere: haze.** Solve the day's and the dusk's haze, which the haze measure does not settle, by the difference between haze that adds and light that scales. Give the cloud sea its two side banks, under 2 metres beneath the walkway some 215 metres to either side, and each emitter's spread from its particle system.
- **Atmosphere: sky.** Solve the dusk's clear sky under its thin cloud and wisps, then check it against a frame that shows the sky beneath. Solve the cover at the horizon, where our haze reads as cloud, and overhead, where no hour but the dusk draws any cloud.
- **Atmosphere: clouds.** Brighten and soften the night's and the day's clouds to the recordings' brightness, solve the day's horizon cloud, and give a cloud its inside: a lit crown grading into a pale shade, with cirrus between.
- **Audio: music.** Layer recordings of real instruments, or a fixed equaliser held out across time, over the synthesizer, since its octave bands sit five to seven decibels off. The first piece's lowest band is overfilled by the noise. The synthesizer's notes die about twice as fast as the game's, and its top voice's overtones are heard as notes, so telling them from a doubled melody is its next unknown.
- **Audio: sounds.** Match the clicks on the screen's buttons, the door's assembly sound and the wind to the game's own sounds, each fitted by its octave bands and started when the game starts it, as the door's opening is.

## The radiance table

Both frames are resized to the display width by the same sharp lanczos3 kernel. Ours is shot with the bloom off, at the reference's recorded camera where it records one and the scene's camera otherwise, as `compare` shoots it. Families are the exports' parts drawn over the reference's scored region, and a pixel is lit where our render's sun-shadow target reaches it above a half. ## Scope

Every pass has its measure in `genshin:parity passes` ([parity](../../specs/genshin/parity/README.md)), and each run it owes is an item of the [compute queue](../../specs/genshin/compute/README.md). What the passes still lack is in two of them, the display's bloom and the atmosphere's cloud layer, in this order:

```text
packages/genshin-engine/src/models/nodes/GameBloomNode.ts
```

1. **Display's bloom, drawn as the game's programs draw it.** A TSL node, `GameBloomNode.ts`, follows the bloom programs `Login/Scene/Display.reference.ts` reads: the prefilter takes `max(0, colour - threshold) * scaler` of the scene colour, the chain box-downsamples by four taps and blurs by four taps down its levels, and the compose sums the four levels by the profile's weights 0.3, 0.3, 0.26 and 0.15, times the intensity, onto the scene colour before the white balance and the tone curve (`toneMapGenshinNode`). Its threshold, scaler, intensity and weights are constants beside `GENSHIN_TONE_CONTRAST` in `packages/genshin-engine/src/renderer/constants.ts`, valued as the Decisions read them. `createPostPipeline` draws it in place of three's `BloomNode` wherever `isBloomed` holds, and `Login/Scene/Index.vue` turns `isBloomed` on. It is proved by one `[page]` compute-queue item the build queues: `pnpm -C scripts genshin:parity passes login --pass Display` still holds its contrast gate, and `compare` on each login reference reads a mean no worse than its row in `ParityReferenceMap.snapshot.md`.
2. **The bloom's own reading.** `measureDisplay` gains a reading around the brightest pixels: on each current build's reference whose screen draws the bloom (`BLOOM_SCREENS` in `passes/constants.ts`, the login's), the pixels within 32 of its own pixels of those it shows at the curve's white, our frame against the reference there with the bloom drawn and with it off, gated where the bloom drawn reads closer. A world reference is not read: its chain draws no bloom, so its frame with the bloom drawn and with it off is one render, and the reading would hold at zero without measuring anything. A miss reopens the field reading in the Decisions.
3. **Atmosphere's cloud layer.** The cloud layer's settings are solved on its statistics over the game's own textures, drawn over textures of ours, though no hour's settings hold every gate yet.

Everything the passes already have is reused as it is, and each measure, once built, retires its row's gap in the toolbox.
