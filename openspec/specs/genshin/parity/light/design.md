# Parity light

The light pass recovers how the game lights its stone, from the exports the reference shows.

- **The stone's light is solved by regression, never searched.** `calibrate` solves a reference's stone light as the game's deferred pass casts it. Each interior pixel's reference colour is taken back into scene colour, less the glow and rim its material adds after lighting, leaving the part the haze lets through: the material's albedo times the toon ramp at its coordinate (facing the sun, scaled by its visibility from the witness's shadow target), plus the sky's spherical harmonics at its normal, plus a light fading with height over the ground by `STONE_HEIGHT_FALLOFF`, all darkened by the scene's occlusion there, plus the stone's haze colour blended toward its sunward one by the scene's scatter at the scene's haze opacity. The model is linear in the ramp's knots, the harmonics' colours, that light's colour and the haze's two. Pixels are binned by part, depth, height, ramp coordinate and how far a face turns up, because the exports' texels do not line up with the reference's. The solve is non-negative least squares over the bins' means, over lights that can be: the ramp rises from none at its dark end without falling, its bends are held toward a straight line, the sky is a sum of lights from directions spread over the sphere, each lighting no face below none, and the height fall-off and haze colours are none or more. Those constraints are the reason rather than a tuning: solved free, the lights cancelled one another, a fading light went red below none, and each tower took a colour rising through its height. Each part's bins weigh the same, so a thin far tower counts as much as the walkway. The solve prints its residual beside the bins' spread; `--write` publishes the light in the world's `login/stoneLight` record under the reference's hour. `--self` solves against the exports as our scene draws them under its own light, a check that the solve models the renderer: it hands the light back within a small margin, and it is what found the haze's blend, the rim's glow and the grade and bloom after tone mapping, each of which had skewed the light. The haze over the stone has colours of its own, solved with the light, while the cloud sea keeps the colours measured where it shows them. A bin under thirty pixels is too few to read and is dropped, and a solve left with none fails rather than hand back a light of none.
