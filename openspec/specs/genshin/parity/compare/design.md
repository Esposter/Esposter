# Parity comparison

`compare` shoots a screen and scores it against its reference. The scores are the record a change is judged by.

- **The comparison is numbers first.** `compare` shoots the screen in the machine's own Edge at 1080 CSS pixels high, scaled to the reference's pixel size. It prints the mean difference and a six-by-six grid of per-cell differences, then writes reference, ours and their difference side by side. A cell that stands out says where to zoom. It prints two scores beside them for a scene, which is rebuilt from shapes rather than copied and so never matches pixel for pixel: the shape, as the share of edges each image has where the other has one too, and the tone, as the difference of their colours blurred past any texture. A reference can hand its screen props of its own, so one screen is judged in each state a reference shows, such as a scene at each time of day.

- **A reference scores its own pixels.** Its `region` is the part scored, its `mask` the rectangles inside the region that count, and its `silhouettes` the glyphs inside it whose strokes alone count, so a map's name scores its letters and never the painted terrain between them. Each glyph is split from its ground in the reference's own pixels, every pixel going to the nearer of the glyph's colour, read off its strokes, and its box's median colour (`splitGlyph`). Splitting from the colour at a box's corners, as `trace` splits a mark on a plain ground, took a map's water for ink, since water stands further from the terrain than a white name does. The ground is still open: a box whose glyph covers more than half of it takes that glyph's ink for ground under the whole-box median, and under the median of its border ring too (measured on the committed Jueyun label: 78% of its box near the glyph's colour, 59% of its ring), so the estimator waits on a re-measure of the gate against frames.

- **FLIP is the approval number.** NVIDIA's standard dynamic range FLIP is ported as it stands (`scoreFlip`): contrast-sensitivity filtering in YCxCz at the viewing distance's pixels per degree, the HyAB distance in Hunt-adjusted CIELab, and the luminance's edges and points, each pixel's error the colour difference raised to one less the feature difference. Its test holds it to NVIDIA's own evaluator on FLIP's example pair. `compare` reports it beside the mean, read at the structure's width as on a full screen. With `--witness`, `compare` also scores each layer apart (`scoreLayers`): a family's pixels in the witness's part target, and the sky where no part is, each with its own shape, tone, detail and mean FLIP, so a change is judged where it lands. `rank` then prices each term of the frame's error, each stand-in against the exports among them.

- **The scores are committed, as a bench's are.** Every `compare` rewrites its reference's row of `ParityReferenceMap.snapshot.md`, beside the map, and `compare --all` rewrites every row it can measure. A reference whose images are not on disk yet keeps its row, and one whose comparison throws is reported as `failed` while the run goes on. A change's diff then shows what it moved on every screen, and the map's test holds every reference to a screen the page shoots. A scene's reference also rewrites its rows of the report's layers table, so a stand-in drawn away from the game shows on its own row even where the frame's score barely moves.

- **Nothing is approved uncompared.** The visual suite only holds a screen to its own last image, so it says nothing about the game. What does is `compare` against a reference in the same language and build, and a test fails for any screen with a fixture that no reference names. A title logo sized from another language's client was approved once without a reference and came out nearly twice the size of the English client's, which is why the test exists. A public video that letterboxes the game is cropped to its screen by the reference's `crop`. A reference recorded in another language says so in its props, and the parity page then gives a screen that shows the game's words that language's text, so the frame is compared in its client's own words.
