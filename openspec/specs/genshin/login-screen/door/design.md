# Login door

The door the glide comes to rest at: it assembles at the walkway's far end as the glide carries it, lights when clicked, and the camera rushes to it as the screen whitens. The click waits until the door has formed.

## How it works

- **The door rides the walkway's far copy, and rises as the glide carries it.** The title's frames show the walkway running on with no door on it. Once the door is due, it rides on the copy the glide comes to rest on, and nothing past that copy is built. It rises as the glide carries it within the walkway's assembling end, as the last blocks settle. The walkway assembles only by its distance from the camera, so its blocks and the door move exactly as the glide does (`getLoginDoorPosition`).
- **The glide keeps its pace until the door's copy is in reach.** Once the door is due the glide keeps its pace, never faster, until the first copy at least the walkway's far end away has come within it. The door rises there and the glide then slows evenly to rest on that copy (`advanceLoginGlide`). The door's copy therefore stands where the camera's pose has it, and the walkway never speeds up to meet a deadline.
- **The door is built from 13 rigid pieces.** Its mesh's bones carry seven pieces of the frame and three of each leaf of the panel (`fitRigidPieces`). Each rises and turns into place from about 6 metres below, along its own path through the game's `Ani_LogginScene_Door01_Liftting` clip (`applyLoginDoorLift`). The first piece settles after 0.4 seconds and the last, the frame's head, after 1.33 seconds. The scene's playback follows the clip's path within a centimetre at its pace, and the door is formed once the last piece has settled (`computeLoginDoorLiftMs`).
- **The door is drawn at no size until it rises.** Until it rises it is drawn at no size and never culled, so its materials' pipelines compile while the scene loads. Mounted only once due, the materials compiled on the frame the door rose and stalled it.
- **The click waits for the door.** The scene says when its door has formed (`doorFormed`), and only then does a click open it and the door's interface come: the four corner buttons 0.3 seconds after, and the prompt's band fading in from 1 second over 0.3 seconds ([interface](../interface/design.md)). A door still rising misses the click.
- **The click rushes the camera to the door.** While the door lights and the screen whitens, the camera closes 41% of its distance to the door in the first third of a second, gathering speed with the square of the time, as the recording's door grows about 1.7 times. It stops short of the door under the white.
- **The door's shape is its mesh's front, layered.** Each piece's frame and panel are the face the game's mesh draws seen from the front, traced and extruded through the part's own depth, so the shouldered, pointed head is the game's. The fit reads the front from above as a grid of depths and keeps each depth enough of the front stands at: the frame's face, the chamfer leaning down to its opening and the panel's raised and bevelled bands. Each layer stands out from the one under it, its top flat and its wall leaning down to the layer below where the mesh leans it (`fitReliefLayers`, `createLoginDoorGeometry`).
- **The layers are built per piece, not per door.** Each rising piece is its own layers at the depths its part's flat faces stand at, its walls leaning over the whole door's front rather than into the cut beside it. At rest the door is the shape the mesh is, to within a millimetre on average. Fitted one piece at a time, the depths a slope steps through on another piece and the leans across a long side piece were lost, and the side chamfers stood flat.
- **The front is painted in the tones its texture carries.** The texture's colours, past their speckle, are grouped into three tones by k-means in CIELab. The tone most of the front shows is drawn under the others, and the feet's gilding is a set of smooth loops in its own colour over the stone (`fitLoginDoor`, `fitPlanTones`). The tones are drawn once and read on each frame's and panel's faces turned to the camera (`createLoginDoorRelief`, `createPlanTonesNode`). The texture paints the frame paler than the panel and a pale edge along every groove; three tones carry those where two did not.
- **The frame and the panel have their own glow.** The frame's material glows at a power of 3, at an eighth of its strength where the frame shows that share (`GLOW_SCALES` in `fitLoginStone`). The panel's material compiles no rim glow, so it draws none. Each is fitted as a family of its own rather than at their mean ([light](../light/design.md)).
- **The door lights from its middle.** A bright line runs down its panel over a glow across the whole door, raised over the door's light time.
- **The exported door's copies stand at the family's offset, every frame.** Each copy keeps its placement within its family and is placed each frame by the family group's current offset, taken through our door at rest (`rideLoginWitnessDoor`, `followLoginWitnessDoor`), as the walkway's and the towers' copies are. Ridden once when the exports or the door's state change, placed every frame, so a witness view's family offset holds. A decision, provisional until the eye-check: per frame rather than read once at ride time, to match the walkway and the towers.
- **The door casts no shadow.** The old dusk sun stood low behind it, so its shadow would have lain down the walkway toward the camera, where the recording's walkway and wings are lit, and the references scored better without it. The solved sun stands higher and the door's shadow is not measured yet ([light](../light/design.md)).
