# Paimon menu

The game's pause menu, laid out as the English PC client's 1.3 still draws it at 2560 wide: its side bar down the left, from Back to Quit Game, and its contents beside it, each in the game's own order and every label the game's own text. Every icon is traced from that still into a path of our own, and an icon it draws in more than one tone into a layer for each, its paler tones laid under its white in their share of it. Community, Version Highlights, Feedback and Survey, the game's links out to web pages, are drawn disabled at the foot of the contents: the still draws Community and Feedback, and the 2026 frame at 25 seconds of `yt-jHr2bERjkYc` draws Version Highlights and Survey beside them. Special Event, which neither reference draws, is left out. Training Guide is left out too, since no still of the menu the references hold draws it.

- **Every entry the still draws, an unbuilt one disabled.** An entry opens its screen once that screen is built, which is whether `MenuScreen` has a slot for it; until then it is drawn disabled. A disabled entry says the game has the screen and this world does not yet, while a missing one would say the game has no such screen.
- **Back closes it, Quit Game asks first.** Quit Game opens the quit prompt over the menu, which stays visible behind it dimmed and inert while it shows. Cancel closes the prompt back to the menu, and Confirm leaves the world for the app's home, since leaving the page is the browser's closing of the game.
- **It opens and closes in 0.26 seconds.** The side bar fades in and the panel a tenth of a second later, on the same curve, and Back closes the menu in reverse, the panel first. The clip the timing is read from (`yt-IegFADn-Lmk-menu-paimon`, the English PC client at 60 frames a second) puts the opening's side bar at half at 35.83 seconds, from 35.75 to 35.91, and its panel done by 36.02; the closing's panel is gone by 110.02 and the side bar's half at 110.07, from 109.98 to 110.15. The fade's curve is a smoothstep, which fits the side bar's alpha with an RMS error of 0.011 opening and 0.006 closing, and its CSS form is the nearest cubic bezier. The HUD vanishes in about 0.05 seconds at 35.43 and returns over 110.07 to 110.48. Quit Game is not animated: its prompt replaces the menu at once.
- **Its side bar lights under the pointer.** Each side bar button is a Disc ([interface library](../interface-library/design.md)): its gold icon turns cream and grows on a navy disc, its name shown beside it over the panel, and pressed the disc turns cream under an ink icon at its resting size. The info icon has no state of its own, as the game draws it. Its panel's entries are Rows, tinted under the pointer by the provisional tints of the [interface library](../interface-library/design.md).
- **Its layout is in the reference's own pixels.** Every place is read off the still by `--reference-pixel`, one pixel of the 2560 wide still at any window's size, and every glyph is placed the same way. Scored over the side bar and the panel above their translucent feet, the still's mean difference is about five percent, its FLIP about 0.22. What is left is the card's stars, the game's typeface where Signika stands in, the translucent foot of the panel where the world shows through, and the Traveler's avatar, which is the game's art and so drawn as a plain disc.
- **The quit prompt is the 2026 build's two choices over the dimmed menu.** A dialog over the menu, which stays visible behind it under a 48% backdrop, the World Level dialog's own: the cream panel is 960 by 606 reference pixels, centred, with the question across its top and two slate pills, 390 by 67 and 70 pixels in from the panel's sides. Cancel carries a blue cross and Confirm a yellow ring, each at its pill's left with its label centred in the rest. The colours are sampled from the 2026 recording at 30 seconds (`yt-jHr2bERjkYc`), and the icons are drawn as shapes, since their paths are not traced. The gold corners and the ornament inside the panel are not drawn, and the prompt is not yet scored over its frame. Hovered and pressed follow the interface library's Row kind.
- **The Settings screen is scored whole over the world.** Its tabs run down the left in the game's order, and the Graphics and Audio tabs draw their rows under their headings, each label the game's own words, laid out from a public recording of the English PC client at 1920 wide (`yt-5EmJX_RyuY8-menu-settings`), where a unit is a pixel. A reference's `plate` fills each of the game's pieces from the blurred world around it ([clean plate](../parity/clean-plate/design.md)), so the frame is scored whole and every piece over what lies behind it: the Graphics tab at its top is 1.18%, FLIP 0.0983 (`settings-graphics-rows`, 144.5 seconds), and the Audio tab 1.15%, FLIP 0.0786 (`settings-audio-rows`, 178 seconds, shot with the pointer resting on Output Settings as the recording's does). Both are approved by their mean difference under 2%, since FLIP reads the typeface's every edge. The wiki's 1680-wide still, scored over its header band, is 1.48%, FLIP 0.0979 (`settings-graphics`). What is left is the game's typeface where Signika stands in and the sparkles drifting along the open tab's light. Only the graphics quality is the world's, its words marking the device's own tier as the default as the game's do; every other row draws the value the recording shows, disabled, as this menu draws an entry whose screen is not built, and so do the tabs past Graphics and Audio. Its close is a Disc and its tabs are Rows, so they light as the [interface library](../interface-library/design.md) sets out. A row lights with a white rim round its edge under the pointer or while it holds the keyboard's focus, as the recording's rows light one at a time from 176 to 179 seconds. The world does not yet open the screen.
- **Its profile card shows the rank and the World Level.** Each value stands right-aligned beside its row's end, where the current client draws it, and the info icon beside the World Level opens the World Level dialog over the dimmed menu ([Adventure Rank](../adventure-rank/design.md)). Birthday stands over an empty value until the profile is backed; the [menu screens](../../../changes/genshin-menu-paimon/design.md) proposal builds it.
- **The World Level dialog is measured off a public recording.** Its frame, 1078 by 716 and centred, its band, line and fill, the title, the cross, the dividers, the scroll track, the body's lines 32 units apart at a cap height of 20 and spaced out to the game's line widths, and the cream button with its red arrow are read from a tutorial recorded at 1080 high, at 5 seconds (`world-level-dialog`), its glyphs traced from the same tutorial's 4K recording. Scored over its frame, the mean difference is about six percent. What is left is the build's wording and the game's typeface: the recording's help text reads World Level 5 and "reduce" where the current text map reads 3 and "decrease", which wraps one line more and moves every line above it.

## Decisions

- **The Settings screen's looks are provisional.** They are read through the world blurred behind them at 144.5 and 178 seconds of `captures/yt-5EmJX_RyuY8-menu-settings.mp4`, each solved from the two worlds the stills show it over: the header's dark veil and how it fades, the light trailing the open tab's name, the faint second star behind each value, the rows' faint shadow and their lit rim, the scroll track's and thumb's alphas, and the Audio tab's speaker, number box and slider. Anisotropic Filtering's 8x is written as a number, since no text id holds it. They stand if the screen reads the same beside those frames in English and 8x stands alike in every language, and any look or that value differing would change it.

- **Back has no state of its own, provisionally.** No recording shows Back under the pointer, so it is drawn as the game draws the info icon, the same in every state, and a keyboard's focus rings it in white. A recording of Back hovered and pressed settles it.

## Notes

- **The agent console's world gets the menu too.** The console mounts the same world, so once it is shown again its own `Escape` and the Paimon menu's both answer the key; its rebuild in the game's style settles which one keeps it.
